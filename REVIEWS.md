# More reviews, collected by Will Durant

*I spent my life introducing great minds to readers who had no time to meet them all. Here are a few more of them, trying out a new instrument. Some approve. Some would have argued. I have let them speak.*

### Laozi - philosopher

*He left us a short book about knowing when to stop. Most readers have been adding to it ever since.*

> The keeper of the pass asked me to write it all down before I left. I gave him five thousand characters and rode away. Soulvant keeps what I wrote, and helps each reader find what matters.

### The Buddha - teacher

*He taught that nothing lasts, and still found a use for a record.*

> My views change, as all things do. Soulvant keeps each old view beside the reason I let it go, so I neither cling to it nor forget what it taught me.

### Confucius - teacher

*He held that a person is made by long practice. His students wrote the practice down for him.*

> At fifteen I set my heart on learning. At seventy I could follow my heart without going astray. Soulvant keeps the fifty-five years in between, so my students can see how I got there.

### Socrates - philosopher

*He left no books. We know him through the people who could not stop writing about him.*

> I never wrote anything down. I thought writing made people forgetful. Soulvant writes everything down and then questions me about it. Plato would have been out of a job.

### Maya Angelou - writer

*She made readers care about a life by being particular about her own.*

> I wrote my life in seven books. Soulvant keeps what I have already told, so each new one can say what the last could not. I still bring the legal pad and the sherry.

### Abraham Lincoln - president

*He had a gift for making himself understood, and a long search for a general who would act on it.*

> I replaced general after general before I found Grant. Thanks to Soulvant, each new one arrived already knowing what I wanted, and why the last one was let go.

### Julia Child - cook

*She took the trouble to explain every step, then made you feel you could do it.*

> Every assistant who walks into my kitchen already knows my taste, thanks to Soulvant: real butter, a sharp knife, and a glass of wine for the cook. I never explain myself twice.

### Benjamin Franklin - printer and statesman

*He approached his own character as he approached an invention: try it, keep a record, and see where it failed.*

> Each morning I asked, "What good shall I do this day?" and each night, "What good have I done?" Soulvant asks me both, then shows me which of my thirteen virtues collected the most black marks this month. It is usually Order.

### Christopher Wren - architect

*A city needed rebuilding. Each parish had its own ideas about where to begin.*

> After the Great Fire I had fifty-one churches and a cathedral to raise at once. Soulvant kept every site, every budget and every bishop's opinion straight. If you seek my monument, look around. If you seek my reasons, ask it.

### William Shakespeare - playwright

*He wrote for a company that needed another play, and gave it more than it knew to ask for.*

> I borrowed every plot worth borrowing. Soulvant keeps Plutarch, Holinshed and every part I wrote for Burbage, so when the company needs a new tragedy by Thursday, we start at the third act.

### Queen Victoria - mother of nine

*She ruled an empire for sixty-three years, and found her family harder to manage.*

> I wrote two thousand words a day in my journal and married nine children into half the thrones of Europe. Soulvant remembers every grandchild's birthday, every in-law's grudge, and which kingdoms are not speaking this week.

### Charles Darwin - naturalist

*He sat on his theory for twenty years, collecting the objections before anyone else could raise them.*

> My golden rule was to write down every fact against my theory at once, because those were the ones I forgot. Soulvant keeps them in front of me. Wallace's letter still came. But this time my notebooks were in order.

### Grace Hopper - programmer

*She expected a machine to follow instructions, and the people giving them to explain themselves.*

> Before an agent changes my code, Soulvant gives it the decisions behind it: what we tried, what failed, and why that peculiar line is still there. It may still disagree. At least we can have a new argument.

### Thomas Edison - inventor

*He gave invention a workshop, a staff, and another experiment to run in the morning.*

> I ran an invention factory with a crew of muckers working through the night. Now the muckers are agents, and Soulvant starts each one with the filaments we already burned out, and what we learned from them.

### Dwight D. Eisenhower - general and president

*He held a coalition of proud allies together long enough to cross the Channel.*

> Plans are worthless, but planning is everything. For D-Day Soulvant kept every assumption and who owned it. When the weather turned, we knew within the hour what had broken.

### Will and Ariel Durant - historians

*We spent our lives introducing other people. Perhaps we may be allowed a word ourselves.*

> Ariel and I spent forty years on eleven volumes. Soulvant remembers not just our conclusions but why we reached them, and what later made us doubt them. It might have saved us from writing the same book twice.
